Case Law on International Crimes

CLIC is a research database documenting how domestic courts apply and interpret international criminal law. It covers both a case law database and a legislation database.

What is CLIC

CLIC — Case Law on International Crimes — is a specialised database documenting decisions by domestic courts that engage with international criminal law. Its focus is on how national jurisdictions apply, interpret, and implement the core crimes under international law: genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and the crime of aggression.

While criminal proceedings form the backbone of the database, CLIC is not limited to them. Cases from other areas of law are included where they bear directly on the core crimes — for example, constitutional law cases examining whether domestic implementing legislation conforms with international treaty obligations.

Scope
Case Law Database
Decisions by domestic courts applying and interpreting international criminal law. Each case record includes the country, court, decision date, crime or issue category, and links to the abstract and full judgment.

Legislation Database
Domestic implementing legislation for each core crime, organised by country. Displays the relevant statutory provisions side-by-side in the original language and English translation where available.

Core Crimes Covered

The database centres on the four core crimes under international law as defined primarily by the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court. Cases are tagged by crime category, and a single case may involve multiple categories.

What Each Case Page Contains
Abstract (English)
A summary of the court's core findings and legal reasoning, prepared in English regardless of the original language of the judgment. The abstract focuses on the court's interpretation of the relevant international criminal law concepts and the outcome of the proceedings.
Full Judgment (Original Language)
A link to the complete court decision in its original language as a PDF. Where an official translation exists, it is linked separately.
Case Metadata
Country, court, decision date, official decision number, crime or issue categories, and source link. Cases are cross-referenced so they can be found both via the country index and via the crime category index.
